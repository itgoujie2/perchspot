# Roof & Roofing Systems Knowledge

## Roofing Material Types and Lifespans

### Asphalt Shingles
**Lifespan**: 15-30 years depending on quality and climate
- **3-Tab Shingles**: 15-20 years (basic, most affordable)
- **Architectural Shingles**: 22-30 years (dimensional, thicker, more durable)
- **Luxury/Premium Shingles**: 30+ years (multi-dimensional, designed to mimic slate/cedar)

**Construction**: Fiberglass or organic base coated with asphalt, covered with mineral granules for UV protection

**Cost**: $4 per square foot on average, $4,200-7,900 for 1,500 sq ft roof (can range $3,000-15,000)

**Weight**: Lightweight compared to tile/slate

**Climate Performance**:
- Works in all climates across US
- Arizona/Desert: Shorter lifespan due to extreme heat and dramatic temperature changes causing cracking
- Northern climates: Performs adequately but requires proper ventilation to prevent ice dams
- Coastal: Salt air can accelerate degradation

**Advantages**:
- Most affordable roofing material
- Easiest to install and repair
- Wide variety of colors and styles
- Readily available
- Suitable for DIY or any licensed roofer
- Lightweight - no structural reinforcement needed

**Disadvantages**:
- Shortest lifespan of major roofing materials
- Less durable than metal/tile/slate
- Susceptible to wind damage (except luxury rated shingles)
- Can soften in extreme heat
- Prone to algae/moss growth in humid climates
- Granule loss over time reduces effectiveness
- Not as weather-resistant as premium materials

**Warranty**: Typically 20-50 years from manufacturer, often prorated after 5-10 years. "Lifetime" warranties usually mean 50 years.

**Maintenance Needs**:
- Replace individual damaged shingles
- Clean gutters to prevent backup
- Remove moss/algae growth
- Inspect after severe weather

---

### Metal Roofing
**Lifespan**: 40-70+ years depending on metal type and thickness
- **Steel (galvanized zinc or aluminum coating)**: 40-50 years
- **Aluminum**: 50+ years
- **Copper**: 100+ years
- **Zinc**: 100+ years
- **Thin metals (26-29 gauge)**: 20-25 years
- **Thick metals (22-24 gauge)**: 50-70+ years

**Types**:
- **Standing Seam**: Panels locked at seams with clips allowing thermal expansion/contraction (recommended for residential)
- **Metal Shingles/Tiles**: Individual metal pieces installed like traditional shingles
- **Corrugated Metal**: Wavy panels, more industrial appearance

**Cost**: Approximately double asphalt shingles upfront ($20,000 vs $10,000 for comparable home)

**Climate Performance**:
- Excellent in heavy snow areas - smooth surface allows snow to slide off
- High wind resistance when properly fastened (can withstand high winds)
- Impact resistant (Class 4 rating)
- Reflects heat - energy efficient in hot climates
- Coastal areas: Susceptible to corrosion from sea salt spray

**Advantages**:
- Long lifespan
- Fire resistant (Class A rating)
- Low maintenance
- Energy efficient - reflects sunlight
- Lightweight compared to tile
- Can be installed over old asphalt shingles
- Snow/ice slides off easily
- Recyclable at end of life
- Increases home value

**Disadvantages**:
- Higher upfront cost
- Can dent from large hail (varies by metal type)
- Potential for rust/corrosion (especially steel in coastal areas)
- Can be noisy during rain/hail without proper insulation
- Requires specialized installation
- Expansion/contraction needs proper fastening system

**Warranty**: 30-year paint warranty on finishes, 20 years to lifetime on material depending on type

**Maintenance Needs**:
- Inspect for rust/corrosion
- Check fasteners and seams
- Clear debris
- Touch up paint/coating if scratched

---

### Clay Tile Roofing
**Lifespan**: 50-100+ years (can exceed 100 years with proper care)

**Styles**: Mediterranean, Southwestern, flat, fluted, interlocking, barrel tiles. Available in glazed or unglazed finishes.

**Cost**: $15,000-35,000 for 1,500 sq ft roof (significantly more expensive than asphalt)

**Weight**: Very heavy - requires structural reinforcement

**Climate Performance**:
- Excellent for hot, sunny climates
- Not ideal for freeze-thaw cycles (can crack in northern climates)
- Fire resistant
- Wind and hail resistant

**Advantages**:
- Extremely long lifespan
- Low maintenance
- Fire resistant
- Does not rot or deteriorate from insects
- Energy efficient - thermal mass properties
- Retains color well
- Increases property value
- Traditional/elegant appearance

**Disadvantages**:
- Very expensive
- Heavy weight requires structural support
- Can break if walked on
- Difficult installation requires skilled professionals
- May not withstand freezing temperatures
- Individual tiles can crack or break

---

### Concrete Tile Roofing
**Lifespan**: 50 years average

**Construction**: Can be designed to mimic various styles (clay, slate, wood shake)

**Cost**: Similar to clay tile, high-end pricing

**Weight**: Heavy like clay - structural support required

**Climate Performance**:
- Handles snow loads well when properly installed
- Fire resistant
- Good for varied climates

**Advantages**:
- Long lifespan
- Versatile appearance options
- Fire resistant
- Low maintenance
- Energy efficient

**Disadvantages**:
- Heavy weight
- Expensive
- Can absorb water in freeze-thaw climates leading to cracks
- Requires structural reinforcement

---

### Slate Roofing
**Lifespan**: 75-150+ years (can last over a century)

**Construction**: Natural slate mined and cut into tiles, installed individually

**Cost**: Premium pricing, one of most expensive roofing materials

**Weight**: Extremely heavy - home must be built or retro-framed to support weight

**Climate Performance**:
- Excellent in all climates
- Does not absorb moisture
- Handles freeze-thaw cycles
- Fire resistant (Class A)
- Wind resistant

**Advantages**:
- Longest lifespan of any roofing material
- Unmatched durability
- Timeless aesthetic appeal
- Fire resistant
- Does not rot, insect-proof
- Low maintenance once installed
- Significantly increases home value
- Weather resistant

**Disadvantages**:
- Most expensive roofing material
- Very heavy - requires structural support verification/reinforcement
- Difficult installation - specialized skills required
- Individual tiles can break if walked on
- Repairs require matching slate which can be expensive

---

### Wood Shingles/Shakes
**Lifespan**: 30-50 years with proper maintenance (typically around 30 years)

**Types**:
- **Shingles**: Machine-cut, uniform
- **Shakes**: Hand-split, rustic appearance

**Cost**: Moderate to high

**Climate Performance**:
- Works in most climates
- Requires treatment for fire resistance
- Not ideal for very wet climates without proper treatment

**Advantages**:
- Natural, rustic appearance
- Good insulation properties
- Can be repaired in sections
- Biodegradable/eco-friendly

**Disadvantages**:
- Susceptible to fire without treatment
- Prone to rot in wet climates
- Insect damage possible
- Requires regular maintenance (treatment, repairs)
- Moss/algae growth in humid areas
- Shorter lifespan than many alternatives
- Higher maintenance requirements

**Warranty**: Up to 40 years depending on treatment and manufacturer

---

### Synthetic/Composite Roofing
**Lifespan**: 30-50+ years (newcomer to industry, long-term data still developing)

**Construction**: Made from recycled plastic, rubber, polymer materials. Engineered to mimic natural materials (slate, cedar shake, tile).

**Cost**: More affordable than natural materials they mimic, but more than basic asphalt

**Climate Performance**:
- Handles freeze-thaw cycles well
- Does not absorb moisture
- Performs consistently across varying conditions
- Lightweight

**Advantages**:
- Long lifespan
- Lighter weight than natural slate/tile
- Less maintenance than natural materials
- Impact resistant
- Won't rot or support moss/algae growth
- Recyclable
- Realistic appearance
- Often 50-year warranties

**Disadvantages**:
- Newer material - less historical performance data
- More expensive than asphalt
- May not match authentic appearance of natural materials
- Quality varies by manufacturer

---

### Copper Roofing
**Lifespan**: 100+ years

**Appearance**: Develops natural patina over time (doesn't affect durability)

**Cost**: Very expensive, premium material

**Advantages**:
- Extremely long lifespan
- No coating or painting needed
- Beautiful patina development
- Increases home value significantly
- Lightweight compared to slate/tile
- Highly resistant to corrosion

**Disadvantages**:
- Very expensive installation
- Requires specialized installation
- Can be loud during rain
- Patina may not appeal to all homeowners

## Roof Structure Components

### Roof Deck/Decking
- **Material**: Usually plywood or OSB (oriented strand board)
- **Function**: Structural foundation supporting all roofing materials
- **Inspection**: Critical to check for soft spots, rot, water damage
- **Lifespan**: Should last life of home if protected from moisture

### Underlayment
- **Materials**: Felt paper, synthetic materials, ice and water shield
- **Function**: Secondary water barrier, protects deck from moisture
- **Importance**: Critical for long-term roof performance

### Rafters and Trusses
- **Function**: Support entire roof structure and loads (snow, wind, materials)
- **Warning Signs**: Cracking, bowing, sagging indicate serious structural issues
- **Load Capacity**: Must be calculated for weight of roofing materials - multiple layers can overload structure

### Ventilation Systems
- **Ridge Vents**: Allow hot air escape at peak
- **Soffit Vents**: Allow cool air intake at eaves
- **Attic Fans**: Powered ventilation assistance
- **Importance**: Prevents moisture buildup, ice dams, extends shingle life, regulates temperature

### Flashing
- **Location**: Around chimneys, vents, skylights, valleys, edges
- **Materials**: Metal (aluminum, copper, galvanized steel) or rubber
- **Function**: Seals vulnerable areas to prevent water intrusion
- **Common Failure Points**: Bending, rust, separation, cracked caulking

### Gutters and Downspouts
- **Function**: Direct water away from foundation
- **Sizing**: Must handle roof runoff volume
- **Maintenance**: Clear debris regularly, check for clogs
- **Termination**: Should direct water at least 6 feet from foundation
- **Problems**: Sagging, rust, poor attachment, improper slope

## Roof Pitch and Design

### Roof Pitch
- **Definition**: Steepness of roof, expressed as rise over run (e.g., 4:12 means 4 inches rise per 12 inches horizontal)
- **Low Pitch** (2:12 to 4:12): May require special materials, more prone to water pooling
- **Standard Pitch** (4:12 to 9:12): Works with most roofing materials
- **Steep Pitch** (9:12+): Better water/snow shedding, more expensive installation

### Roof Shapes
- **Gable**: Two slopes meeting at ridge (most common)
- **Hip**: Four slopes meeting at peak
- **Flat**: Minimal slope, requires special waterproofing
- **Mansard**: Four sides with two slopes each
- **Gambrel**: Barn-style with two different pitch angles

Complexity of roof shape affects installation cost and maintenance requirements.

## Climate-Specific Considerations

### Hot/Sunny Climates (Arizona, Texas, Florida)
- **Best Materials**: Clay tile, concrete tile, metal (reflective), light-colored shingles
- **Issues**: UV damage, thermal cycling, asphalt shingle degradation
- **Features**: Cool roof technology, reflective coatings

### Cold/Snowy Climates (Northeast, Midwest)
- **Best Materials**: Metal (snow slides off), slate, architectural shingles
- **Issues**: Ice dams, freeze-thaw cycles, heavy snow loads
- **Features**: Ice and water shield, proper ventilation, adequate insulation
- **Frost Line**: Deeper frost requires proper support structure

### Humid/Rainy Climates (Pacific Northwest, Southeast)
- **Best Materials**: Metal, composite, treated wood
- **Issues**: Moss/algae growth, rot, moisture accumulation
- **Features**: Algae-resistant shingles, proper ventilation, steep pitch for drainage

### High Wind Areas (Coastal, Tornado Alley)
- **Best Materials**: Metal with proper fastening, luxury shingles rated for high winds
- **Ratings**: Look for wind resistance ratings (up to 130 mph for premium products)
- **Installation**: Proper attachment critical

### Hail-Prone Regions
- **Best Materials**: Impact-resistant shingles (Class 4 rating), metal roofing
- **Considerations**: Check insurance requirements

## Inspection Checklist

### Exterior Inspection (from ground/ladder)
- Missing, damaged, curling, or cracked shingles/tiles
- Sagging roof lines indicating structural issues
- Moss, algae, or debris accumulation
- Condition of flashing (cracks, rust, gaps)
- Chimney condition (crumbling mortar, cracks)
- Gutter/downspout condition and attachment
- Pooling water on flat sections
- Damaged or missing roof penetration seals

### Interior Inspection (attic)
- Water stains on ceiling or rafters
- Daylight visible through roof boards
- Sagging or damaged decking
- Damp or wet insulation
- Mold or mildew growth
- Musty odors
- Proper ventilation operation
- Rafter/truss condition (cracks, bowing)

### Specific Warning Signs
- **Granule Loss**: Asphalt shingles shedding granules (check gutters)
- **Curling Edges**: Shingles lifting at edges
- **Blistering**: Bubbles in shingles from trapped moisture
- **Rust/Corrosion**: Metal components deteriorating
- **Soft Spots**: Walking on roof reveals spongy areas (deck rot)

## Maintenance Requirements by Material

### Asphalt Shingles
- **Frequency**: Biannual inspections (spring and fall)
- **Tasks**: Replace damaged shingles, clean gutters, remove debris, check flashing
- **Professional Inspection**: Every 3-5 years or after severe weather

### Metal Roofing
- **Frequency**: Annual inspection
- **Tasks**: Check for rust, inspect fasteners, clear debris, verify sealants intact
- **Professional Inspection**: Every 5-10 years

### Tile (Clay/Concrete)
- **Frequency**: Annual inspection
- **Tasks**: Replace broken tiles, check underlayment condition, clear debris
- **Professional Inspection**: Every 5-10 years
- **Special Care**: Avoid walking on tiles

### Slate
- **Frequency**: Annual inspection from ground, professional every 10-20 years
- **Tasks**: Replace broken slates, check flashing, clear gutters
- **Special Care**: Only professionals should walk on slate

## Roof Replacement Indicators

### Age-Based
- Asphalt: Consider replacement at 15-20 years
- Metal: Inspect carefully at 40+ years
- Tile/Slate: May never need full replacement, individual repairs only

### Condition-Based
- Multiple leaks
- Widespread shingle damage (20%+ of roof)
- Sagging roof deck
- Daylight visible from attic
- Energy bills increasing (poor insulation/ventilation)
- Mold growth in attic
- Multiple layers of old roofing (building codes often limit to 2 layers)

---

## Special Roof Features

### Skylights
- Potential leak points requiring careful flashing
- Regular seal inspection needed
- Can increase natural light but may increase heat gain/loss

### Roof Valleys
- Where two roof planes meet
- Critical waterproofing area
- Can use metal valley flashing or woven/cut shingle valleys
- Higher wear from concentrated water flow

### Chimneys
- Requires step flashing and counter flashing
- Mortar crown should be intact
- Cricket (diverter) recommended on upslope side
- Regular inspection for deterioration

### Solar Panels
- Add attachment points requiring proper sealing
- May void some roofing warranties
- Complicate future roof replacement
- Can extend roof life by shading shingles beneath

---

## Roof Color and Energy Efficiency

### Light Colors
- Reflect more sunlight
- Keep structure cooler
- Reduce cooling costs in hot climates
- Extend lifespan of roofing materials in hot areas

### Dark Colors
- Absorb heat
- Can help in cold climates
- May cause overheating, poor ventilation, premature aging in hot climates

### Cool Roof Technology
- Special reflective coatings
- Can lower roof temperature 50-60°F
- Reduce air conditioning demand
- Available in various colors

## Regional Building Code Considerations

### Wind Load Requirements
- Hurricane zones require enhanced fastening
- Specific wind speed ratings
- May require structural engineer certification

### Fire Ratings
- Wildfire-prone areas may require Class A fire-rated materials
- Some materials prohibited in high-fire zones

### Snow Load
- Northern regions require calculations for snow weight
- Affects rafter/truss sizing
- Influences material selection

---

## Warranty Considerations

### Material Warranty
- Covers defects in manufacturing
- Typically 20-50 years
- Often prorated after initial period (5-10 years)
- Does not cover installation issues

### Workmanship Warranty
- Covers installation errors
- Usually shorter term (1-10 years)
- Provided by contractor, not manufacturer
- Important to verify contractor licensing and insurance

### Enhanced/System Warranties
- Cover complete roof system when using all manufacturer products
- May extend coverage to 50 years non-prorated
- Often transferable to new homeowner
- Require certified installer

## Age-Based Roof Concerns

### Pre-2000 Roof Replacement Planning
@applies_when: year_built<2000
@priority: high

Homes built before 2000 likely have roofs approaching or past typical replacement age. Asphalt shingles last 15-30 years, so original roofs from pre-2000 are due for inspection or replacement. Budget $8,000-20,000 for asphalt shingle replacement depending on roof size and complexity. Ask seller about roof age and any repairs.

---

### Pre-1995 Roof Structure Check
@applies_when: year_built<1995
@priority: medium

Roofs over 30 years old require careful inspection of underlying structure (decking, rafters, trusses) in addition to surface materials. Multiple roofing layers may be present - building codes often limit to 2 layers. Full tear-off adds $1,000-3,000 to replacement cost.

---

### Pre-1990 Ventilation Assessment
@applies_when: year_built<1990
@priority: medium

Older homes often have inadequate roof ventilation by modern standards. Poor ventilation causes premature shingle aging, ice dams in cold climates, and moisture problems. Adding proper ridge and soffit vents costs $300-600 and significantly extends roof life.

---

### Pre-1980 Roof Inspection Priority
@applies_when: year_built<1980
@priority: high

Homes over 45 years old may have had multiple roof replacements. Verify roof history and check for underlying damage from past leaks. Original roof decking may need replacement ($2-4 per sqft) if it has deteriorated. Inspect attic for water staining and structural issues.
