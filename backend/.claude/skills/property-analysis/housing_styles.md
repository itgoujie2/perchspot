## Classic Style Definitions

### Victorian (1837-1901)
- **Features**: Ornate decoration, high ceilings, bay windows, steep roofs, asymmetrical
- **Queen Anne subtype**: Turrets, wraparound porches, multiple textures, stained glass
- **Quality**: Well-built, may need specialized restoration, high maintenance

### Colonial Revival (1880-Present)
- **Features**: Symmetrical facade, central entrance, evenly spaced windows, gabled/hipped roof, dormers, shutters
- **Subtypes**: Dutch Colonial (gambrel roof), Georgian, Cape Cod, Garrison
- **Quality**: First wave (pre-1945) excellent; Neo-Colonial (post-1945) simpler

### Craftsman / Bungalow (1900-1930)
- **Features**: Low-pitched gabled roof, wide eaves, exposed rafters, tapered porch columns, built-in cabinetry
- **Interior**: Open floor plan, natural woodwork, prominent fireplace
- **Quality**: Excellent craftsmanship, durable construction

### Tudor Revival (1890-1940)
- **Features**: Steep gable roofs, half-timbering with stucco, diamond-pane windows, massive chimneys, arched doors
- **Quality**: Expensive to build/maintain, "Stockbroker Tudor" nickname

### Mid-Century Modern (1945-1970)
- **Features**: Flat/low roofs, large windows, open floor plans, indoor-outdoor integration, minimal ornament
- **Subtypes**: Ranch, Eichler homes, Case Study Houses
- **Quality**: Well-built, highly desirable, may need energy updates

### Ranch (1935-Present)
- **Features**: Single-story, low-pitched roof, attached garage, large windows, sliding glass doors
- **Layouts**: L-shaped, U-shaped, linear
- **Quality**: Good construction, easy single-floor navigation

### Split-Level / Tri-Level (1950-1980)
- **Types**: Bi-level (2 floors), Tri-level (3 floors), Quad-level (4 floors)
- **Features**: Staggered floors, short stair flights, sunken living room, distinct zones
- **Layout**: Main level (kitchen/dining/living), upper (bedrooms), lower (family room/garage)
- **Quality**: Good for sloping lots, stairs can limit accessibility

### Modern Farmhouse (2010s-Present)
- **Exterior**: White siding, gable roofs, metal accents, black window frames, large porches
- **Interior**: Shiplap, neutral palette, open floor plan, industrial accents, exposed beams
- **Quality**: Good construction, energy efficient, broad market appeal

## Quality Assessment by Era

### Pre-1940s
- **Pros**: Quality materials, true dimensional lumber, craftsmanship
- **Watch**: Outdated electrical/plumbing, lead paint, knob-and-tube wiring, foundation issues

### 1940s-1960s
- **Pros**: Standardized construction, FHA guidelines, practical layouts
- **Watch**: Asbestos materials, original systems, limited electrical capacity

### 1970s-1980s
- **Pros**: Energy awareness, larger homes, improved insulation
- **Watch**: Quality variability, dated aesthetics, asbestos, aging HVAC

### 1990s-2000s
- **Pros**: Large spaces, modern systems, customization
- **Watch**: Rushed construction, quality variation, high energy costs, complex roofs

### 2010s-Present
- **Pros**: Energy efficiency, smart home ready, quality finishes
- **Watch**: Premium pricing, trend-specific features, verify builder reputation

## Regional Variations

### Pacific Northwest (Seattle)
- **Common**: Craftsman, Mid-Century Modern, Contemporary, Northwest Contemporary
- **Features**: Natural materials, large windows, covered outdoor spaces
- **Watch**: Moisture issues (rot, mold), drainage, weatherproofing

### Northeast
- **Common**: Colonial Revival, Cape Cod, Victorian, Tudor
- **Features**: Steep roofs for snow, central chimneys, smaller windows

### Southwest
- **Common**: Spanish Colonial, Pueblo Revival, Ranch, Mid-Century Modern
- **Features**: Stucco exteriors, tile roofs, courtyards, flat roofs

### Southeast
- **Common**: Colonial Revival, Greek Revival, Victorian, Lowcountry
- **Features**: Wide porches, raised foundations, ventilation focus

## Key Identification Tips

| If you see... | It's likely... |
|---------------|----------------|
| Turret/tower, ornate trim, wraparound porch | Queen Anne Victorian |
| Symmetrical, central door, shutters | Colonial Revival |
| Low roof, exposed rafters, tapered columns | Craftsman |
| Half-timbering, steep gables, diamond windows | Tudor |
| Steep roof, dormers, simple rectangular | Cape Cod |
| Single-story, long and low, attached garage | Ranch |
| Staggered floor levels, multiple short stairs | Split-Level |
| White exterior, black trim, shiplap interior | Modern Farmhouse |
| Oversized, mixed styles, grand foyer | McMansion |
