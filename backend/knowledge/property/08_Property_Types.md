# Property Type Considerations

## Townhome Shared Wall Considerations
@applies_when: property_type contains townhome
@priority: medium

Townhomes share one or more walls with neighbors - consider noise transfer potential. Look for: STC (Sound Transmission Class) ratings if available, concrete vs. wood construction between units, and location of bedrooms relative to shared walls. Ask about neighbor noise during showing.

---

## Townhome Exterior Maintenance
@applies_when: property_type contains townhome
@priority: medium

Townhome exterior maintenance responsibility varies - some HOAs cover roof and siding, others only common areas. Clarify exactly what the HOA maintains vs. owner responsibility. This significantly impacts both monthly costs and long-term maintenance obligations.

---

## Townhome Insurance Requirements
@applies_when: property_type contains townhome
@priority: low

Townhome insurance (HO-6) differs from single-family coverage. The HOA master policy typically covers the building exterior and common areas, while you need walls-in coverage for interior, personal property, and liability. Review HOA policy to understand coverage gaps.

---

## Condo Special Assessment Risk
@applies_when: property_type contains condo
@priority: high

Condo associations may levy special assessments for major repairs not covered by reserves. Request reserve study, recent meeting minutes, and planned capital improvements. Special assessments of $10,000-50,000+ can occur for elevator replacement, parking garage repairs, or building envelope issues.

---

## Condo Building Systems
@applies_when: property_type contains condo
@priority: medium

In condos, you share major building systems with all owners. Elevator maintenance, HVAC central plant, parking structure, and plumbing risers are common expense items. Older condo buildings (20+ years) may face significant infrastructure updates - review reserve funding.

---

## Condo Association Health
@applies_when: property_type contains condo
@priority: high

Evaluate condo association financial health: reserves should be 70%+ funded, no pending litigation, reasonable delinquency rate (under 10%), and stable or modest fee increases. Request audited financial statements and recent reserve study.

---

## Single Family Maintenance Responsibility
@applies_when: property_type contains single
@priority: low

Single family homes give you full control but also full responsibility for all maintenance. Budget for roof replacement every 20-30 years ($8,000-20,000), HVAC every 15-20 years ($8,000-15,000), exterior painting every 7-10 years ($3,000-8,000), and ongoing landscaping.

---

## Small Lot Property Considerations
@applies_when: lot_size_sqft<4000
@priority: medium

Lots under 4,000 sqft are common in urban areas and townhome developments. Consider: limited outdoor space, neighbor proximity, and potential expansion limitations. Smaller lots mean lower maintenance but may impact resale to buyers wanting more space.

---

## Large Lot Maintenance
@applies_when: lot_size_sqft>15000
@priority: medium

Lots over 15,000 sqft (about 1/3 acre) require significant landscaping maintenance. Budget for lawn care ($2,000-5,000/year if hired out), tree maintenance, potential irrigation systems, and longer driveway/fence maintenance costs.

---

## Multi-Story Accessibility
@applies_when: stories>=3
@priority: low

Three+ story homes present accessibility considerations for aging in place. Stairs become more challenging with age or mobility issues. Consider location of main bedroom, laundry, and daily living spaces. Evaluate whether a stairlift could be installed if needed.

---

## Small Living Space Efficiency
@applies_when: living_area_sqft<1200
@priority: low

Homes under 1,200 sqft require efficient space usage. Evaluate storage options, room configurations, and whether the layout works for your needs. Smaller spaces can be more energy efficient but may feel cramped. Consider future needs if family size may change.

---

## Large Home Operating Costs
@applies_when: living_area_sqft>3500
@priority: medium

Homes over 3,500 sqft have higher operating costs: more to heat/cool ($200-400+/month in extreme seasons), more to clean and maintain, higher property taxes, and more roof/siding to eventually replace. Ensure HVAC system is appropriately sized.
