# Walkability and Transit Score Methodology

## Overview
Walk Score, Transit Score, and Bike Score measure how friendly a location is for walking, public transit, and biking on a scale of 0-100.

## Walk Score (0-100 Scale)

### Score Interpretation
- **90-100 (Walker's Paradise)**: Daily errands do not require a car
- **70-89 (Very Walkable)**: Most errands can be accomplished on foot
- **50-69 (Somewhat Walkable)**: Some errands can be accomplished on foot
- **25-49 (Car-Dependent)**: Most errands require a car
- **0-24 (Car-Dependent)**: Almost all errands require a car

### Calculation Methodology
Walk Score measures walkability based on:
- Distance to nearby amenities (grocery stores, restaurants, schools, parks, etc.)
- Pedestrian friendliness (population density, road metrics, block length)
- Number of amenities within walking distance (5-minute = 0.25 miles, 30-minute = 1.5 miles)

### Key Amenities Evaluated
1. **Grocery Stores** (highest weight) - Within 0.25-1 mile
2. **Restaurants & Coffee Shops** - Dining and social options
3. **Shopping** - Retail, clothing stores
4. **Parks** - Green spaces and recreation
5. **Schools** - Elementary, middle, high schools
6. **Entertainment** - Theaters, museums, venues
7. **Banking** - ATMs and financial services
8. **Books/Media** - Libraries, bookstores

### Distance-Based Scoring
- **0-0.25 miles (5 min walk)**: Maximum points
- **0.25-0.5 miles (10 min walk)**: High points
- **0.5-1.0 miles (20 min walk)**: Moderate points
- **1.0-1.5 miles (30 min walk)**: Low points
- **>1.5 miles**: No points

## Transit Score (0-100 Scale)

### Score Interpretation
- **90-100 (Rider's Paradise)**: Excellent public transportation, convenient for most trips
- **70-89 (Excellent Transit)**: Many nearby public transit options
- **50-69 (Good Transit)**: Many nearby transit options, but may be less frequent
- **25-49 (Some Transit)**: A few public transportation options
- **0-24 (Minimal Transit)**: Very few or no public transit options

### Calculation Methodology
Transit Score is based on:
- Distance to nearest public transit stop
- Type of route (rail, bus, ferry)
- Frequency of service (headways)
- Number of nearby routes

### Transit Type Weighting
1. **Rail/Subway/Metro** (highest weight) - Most reliable, frequent
2. **Light Rail/Streetcar** - Regular service
3. **Ferry** - Scheduled water transit
4. **Bus** - Varies by frequency
5. **Other** (cable car, gondola, etc.)

### Service Frequency Impact
- **<5 minute headways**: Maximum points
- **5-10 minute headways**: High points
- **10-15 minute headways**: Moderate points
- **15-30 minute headways**: Low points
- **>30 minute headways**: Minimal points

## Bike Score (0-100 Scale)

### Score Interpretation
- **90-100 (Biker's Paradise)**: Daily errands can be accomplished on a bike, excellent infrastructure
- **70-89 (Very Bikeable)**: Biking is convenient for most trips
- **50-69 (Bikeable)**: Some bike infrastructure
- **0-49 (Somewhat Bikeable)**: Minimal bike infrastructure

### Calculation Methodology
Bike Score measures:
- Bike infrastructure (bike lanes, trails, paths)
- Topography/hills (flat terrain scores higher)
- Destinations and road connectivity
- Bike commuting mode share

### Infrastructure Types
1. **Protected bike lanes** - Physically separated from traffic
2. **Bike lanes** - Painted lanes on roads
3. **Bike paths/trails** - Off-road dedicated paths
4. **Bike-friendly roads** - Low-speed, low-traffic streets
5. **Bike parking/racks** - Secure parking facilities

## Analysis Guidelines

### Home Value Impact
- **Walk Score 90+**: Can increase property values 5-20% in urban areas
- **Walk Score 70-89**: Moderate positive impact (2-10%)
- **Walk Score <50**: Neutral or negative in urban markets, expected in suburbs
- **Transit Score 70+**: Premium for properties near quality transit (10-25% in major cities)

### Target Scores by Buyer Profile

**Urban Professionals:**
- Walk Score: 80+ required
- Transit Score: 70+ preferred
- Bike Score: 60+ bonus

**Families with Children:**
- Walk Score: 60+ acceptable (schools and parks prioritized)
- Transit Score: 40+ acceptable
- Bike Score: 50+ preferred for kids

**Retirees:**
- Walk Score: 70+ preferred (medical, grocery access)
- Transit Score: 60+ preferred (no driving preference)
- Bike Score: Less important

**Suburban Buyers:**
- Walk Score: 40+ acceptable
- Transit Score: 30+ acceptable
- Bike Score: 40+ nice-to-have

**Investors (Rental Properties):**
- Walk Score: 70+ for urban rentals (higher rents)
- Transit Score: 60+ for urban rentals
- Walk Score: 50+ for suburban family rentals

### Key Questions to Answer
1. What are the Walk, Transit, and Bike scores?
2. What specific amenities are within walking distance?
3. How far to nearest grocery store, park, and school?
4. What public transit options exist and how frequent?
5. Are there bike lanes or paths nearby?
6. How do scores compare to neighborhood/city averages?

## Red Flags
- Walk Score <30 in urban area (poor urban planning)
- Transit Score <20 in major city (limited mobility)
- No grocery store within 1 mile (food desert)
- Steep hills with no transit options (mobility challenges)
- High car dependency despite urban density

## Positive Indicators
- Walk Score 80+ with grocery, restaurants, parks within 0.5 miles
- Transit Score 70+ with multiple rail/subway options
- Bike Score 70+ with protected bike infrastructure
- "15-minute neighborhood" - all essentials within 15-minute walk
- High walkability with low crime (best combination)

## Context for Interpretation

### Urban vs. Suburban Expectations
- **Urban (city center)**: Walk Score 70+ expected, Transit Score 60+ expected
- **Urban periphery**: Walk Score 50-70 typical, Transit Score 40-60 typical
- **Inner suburbs**: Walk Score 40-60 typical, Transit Score 20-40 typical
- **Outer suburbs**: Walk Score 20-40 typical, Transit Score 0-20 typical
- **Rural**: Walk Score <20 expected, Transit Score 0 expected

### Lifestyle Compatibility
**High walkability (80+) best for:**
- Car-free or one-car households
- Young professionals
- Empty nesters
- Environmental consciousness
- Active lifestyle preference

**Moderate walkability (50-70) best for:**
- Families wanting balance
- Hybrid work arrangements
- Some car dependency acceptable

**Low walkability (<50) best for:**
- Large families needing space
- Car enthusiasts
- Home-based work
- Privacy preference
- Lower cost priority

### Cost-Benefit Analysis
**High Walk Score Areas:**
- Pros: Lower transportation costs, healthier lifestyle, higher resale value
- Cons: Higher purchase price, smaller lots, more noise, less parking

**Low Walk Score Areas:**
- Pros: Larger homes, more parking, quieter, lower cost per sq ft
- Cons: Higher transportation costs, car dependency, potentially lower resale

## Reporting Guidelines

### Standard Report Format
```
Walk Score: 85/100 - Very Walkable
- Grocery: 0.3 miles (Whole Foods, Safeway)
- Restaurants: 15+ within 0.5 miles
- Parks: 0.2 miles (Central Park)
- Schools: 0.4 miles (Elementary)

Transit Score: 92/100 - Rider's Paradise
- 2 subway lines within 0.2 miles (Red, Green)
- 5 bus routes within 0.3 miles
- Average wait time: 6 minutes

Bike Score: 78/100 - Very Bikeable
- Protected bike lanes on Main St
- 2 bike paths within 0.5 miles
- Flat terrain, minimal hills
- Bike share station 0.1 miles away

Overall Mobility: Excellent - Daily life easily managed without a car
```

### Comparison Context
- Include neighborhood average scores
- Compare to city average
- Note if scores are improving/declining over time
- Highlight any planned transit or infrastructure improvements

### Investment Considerations
- Higher walk/transit scores = higher rental demand in urban markets
- Lower parking costs for car-free tenants
- Appeal to younger demographics (millennials, Gen Z)
- Consider work-from-home trends reducing transit importance
- Proximity to transit may increase with climate concerns
