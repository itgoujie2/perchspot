# Condition Scoring Rubric

## Year Built Assessment

### Pre-1980
- **Major concerns**: Asbestos insulation/siding, lead paint, galvanized steel plumbing, aluminum wiring (1960s-70s), single-pane windows, inadequate insulation, 100A electrical panels
- **Typical costs**: Full rewire $8-15K, replumb $8-20K, asbestos abatement $5-20K, window replacement $15-30K
- **Score impact**: -15 to -25 points unless renovated

### 1980s–1990s
- **Known issues**: Polybutylene pipes (grey/blue, fail catastrophically — $8-15K to replumb), early composition roofs (25-year life expired), builder-grade everything, some aluminum wiring carry-over
- **PNW-specific**: Many homes built with T1-11 or wood siding that has moisture damage by now
- **Score impact**: -10 to -15 points; verify if plumbing updated

### 2000s
- **Generally solid**: Modern code, PEX plumbing, 200A panels, double-pane windows standard
- **Watch for**: Original roof nearing end of life (20-25 year compo shingles), original water heater (15-year life), original HVAC (15-20 year life)
- **Score impact**: -5 for approaching major system replacements

### 2010–2019
- **Modern construction**: Energy code improvements, better insulation, arc-fault breakers
- **Minimal concerns**: Most systems still within useful life
- **Score impact**: Baseline (no penalty)

### 2020+  / New Construction
- **Latest codes**: WA energy code 2018+, heat pump requirements in some jurisdictions
- **Builder warranty**: Typically 1-year workmanship, 2-year systems, 10-year structural
- **Score impact**: +5 to +10

## Property Type Assessment

### Single Family Home
- **Pros**: Full land ownership, no shared walls, customization freedom, best long-term appreciation, no HOA restrictions (usually)
- **Cons**: Full maintenance responsibility, higher insurance, larger down payment
- **Eastside context**: Preferred by families; 3+ bed SFH is the core market

### Townhome
- **Pros**: Lower entry price, reduced exterior maintenance (HOA handles), newer construction common
- **Cons**: Shared walls (noise), HOA fees and rules, smaller lots, limited customization, slower appreciation vs SFH
- **Eastside context**: Good starter homes; Sammamish Town Center, Issaquah Highlands have quality townhome communities

### Condo
- **Pros**: Lowest entry price, amenities (gym, pool), lock-and-leave lifestyle
- **Cons**: Monthly HOA $300-800+, special assessments risk, appreciation lags SFH by 2-4% annually, HOA financial health critical
- **Eastside context**: Best near transit (light rail in Bellevue); check reserve fund — WA requires reserve studies

### Slope Considerations (PNW-specific)
- Flat to gentle slope: Ideal, no drainage concerns
- Moderate slope: Verify retaining walls, drainage; daylight basements add livable space
- Steep slope: Landslide risk (check City of Sammamish geohazard maps), expensive foundation work, difficult driveway in ice/snow
- Downhill driveway/garage: Drainage critical in Seattle rain — verify French drains, trench drains

## Parking Assessment

| Parking | Score Impact | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| No garage | -15 | Unusual and undesirable on Eastside |
| 1-car garage | -5 | Below market standard |
| 2-car garage | Baseline | Market standard for Eastside |

- **Tandem garage**: Counts as 2 spaces but less practical than side-by-side

**Key checks**: Reserve fund adequacy, pending special assessments, litigation history, rental restrictions

## Missing Data Flags

Always flag when these fields are absent from extracted data:
- **Heating/Cooling**: Critical for PNW (verify if has A/C, heat pump, or forced air only)
- **Exterior material**: Stucco in PNW is a moisture risk; cedar needs maintenance
- **Roof type/age**: Biggest single maintenance expense ($15-30K)
- **Foundation type**: Crawl space moisture, slab cracks
- **Electrical panel capacity**: 100A insufficient for modern needs + EV
- **Plumbing material**: Polybutylene is a dealbreaker if not replaced
