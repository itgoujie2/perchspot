# Home Age Considerations

## Pre-1978 Lead Paint Disclosure
@applies_when: year_built<1978
@priority: high

Federal law requires lead paint disclosure for pre-1978 homes. Lead-based paint was banned in 1978 and is a health hazard, especially for children. Budget for lead paint inspection ($300-500) and potential remediation if paint is deteriorating. Intact lead paint is generally safe if not disturbed.

---

## Pre-1980 Galvanized Pipe Concerns
@applies_when: year_built<1980
@priority: high

Homes built before 1980 often have galvanized steel water pipes that corrode internally over 40-50 years. Signs include low water pressure, rusty water, and frequent leaks. Full repiping costs $8,000-15,000 for a typical home. Check water pressure and look for visible corrosion.

---

## 1978-1995 Polybutylene Pipe Risk
@applies_when: year_built>=1978
@applies_when: year_built<=1995
@priority: high

Polybutylene (PB) pipes were common 1978-1995 and are prone to failure due to chlorine degradation. Gray plastic pipes (stamped PB2110) should be replaced proactively - they can fail without warning causing major water damage. Replacement costs $4,000-12,000.

---

## Pre-1985 Electrical Concerns
@applies_when: year_built<1985
@priority: medium

Homes built before 1985 may have outdated electrical systems: 100-amp service (modern homes need 200-amp), ungrounded outlets, or Federal Pacific/Zinsco panels (known fire hazards). Electrical panel upgrade costs $1,500-4,000. Check for two-prong outlets indicating ungrounded wiring.

---

## Pre-1990 Older Home Maintenance
@applies_when: year_built<1990
@priority: medium

Homes 35+ years old typically need major system updates: HVAC replacement ($8,000-15,000), water heater ($1,500-3,000), and potential roof replacement if original. Budget 1-2% of home value annually for maintenance on older homes vs. 0.5-1% for newer construction.

---

## 1990s Construction Considerations
@applies_when: year_built>=1990
@applies_when: year_built<2000
@priority: medium

1990s homes are reaching the age where major systems need replacement. HVAC systems last 15-20 years, water heaters 10-15 years, and roofs 20-30 years. If these are original, budget for replacement in the near term. Check service records for maintenance history.

---

## 2000s Construction Quality
@applies_when: year_built>=2000
@applies_when: year_built<2010
@priority: low

Homes built 2000-2009 generally have modern systems but may have been built during housing boom with variable quality. Check for signs of deferred maintenance. Most major systems should have 5-15 years of life remaining if original.

---

## Modern Construction Benefits
@applies_when: year_built>=2010
@priority: low

Homes built after 2010 benefit from modern building codes requiring better insulation, energy efficiency, and safety features. Major systems should be in good condition with significant remaining lifespan. Focus inspection on workmanship quality rather than age-related wear.

---

## Historic Home Considerations
@applies_when: year_built<1950
@priority: high

Pre-1950 homes may have unique challenges: knob-and-tube wiring ($8,000-20,000 to replace), cast iron drain pipes (may need replacement), balloon framing (fire safety concern), and potential asbestos insulation. Budget for thorough inspection by specialist in historic homes.
