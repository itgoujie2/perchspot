# GreatSchools Rating Methodology

## Overview
GreatSchools provides a 1-10 score comparing schools within the same state.

## Rating Scale
- **1-4**: Below-average performance
- **5-6**: Average performance
- **7-10**: Above-average performance

## Three Themed Ratings

### 1. Student Progress Rating (Highest Weight)
- Measures year-over-year academic growth
- Compares students to academically similar students statewide
- Uses state-reported student growth data
- Isolates school's contribution to learning
- **Most important indicator** - research shows growth is most reliable measure of school impact

### 2. College Readiness Rating (Moderate Weight)
Multi-measure rating based on:
- College entrance exam performance (SAT and ACT)
- High school graduation rates
- Advanced coursework participation (AP, IB, or dual enrollment)

### 3. Test Score Rating (Moderate Weight)
- Based on state-standardized test performance
- Reflects student achievement levels
- More influenced by external factors like family income

## Weighting Methodology
- **Student Progress Rating**: Highest weight (strength score: 5)
- **College Readiness Rating**: Moderate weight (strength score: 3)
- **Test Score Rating**: Moderate weight (strength score: 3)
- No themed rating can exceed weight of Student Progress Rating

## Analysis Guidelines

When analyzing schools for housing analysis:

### Key Metrics to Report
1. Overall GreatSchools Rating (1-10 scale)
2. Student Progress Rating (most important)
3. College Readiness Rating (for high schools)
4. Test Score Rating (baseline measure)

### Interpretation Framework
- **9-10**: Outstanding school, significant home value premium
- **7-8**: Good school, above-average performance, desirable
- **5-6**: Average school, acceptable but not a selling point
- **3-4**: Below average, may concern families, potential value discount
- **1-2**: Poor performance, major concern for buyers with children

### Distance Considerations
- Elementary school: Ideal within 0.5 miles (walking distance)
- Middle school: Acceptable within 1-2 miles
- High school: Acceptable within 3-5 miles

### Key Questions to Answer
1. What are the overall ratings for elementary, middle, and high schools?
2. How do student progress ratings compare? (growth vs. achievement)
3. For high schools: What are college readiness metrics?
4. How do assigned schools compare to district and state averages?
5. Are there better school options nearby (private, charter, magnet)?

## Red Flags
- Declining student progress ratings over time
- Large gap between test scores and student progress (may indicate external factors)
- Very low college readiness ratings for high schools
- Schools on state improvement/intervention lists

## Positive Indicators
- Consistent or improving student progress ratings
- High college readiness scores (above 7)
- Strong participation in advanced coursework
- Above-average performance across all three ratings
