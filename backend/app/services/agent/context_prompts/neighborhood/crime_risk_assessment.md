# Crime Risk Assessment - NeighborhoodScout Methodology

## Overview
NeighborhoodScout provides instant, objective assessment of property and violent crime risks for every U.S. address with up to 98% accuracy.

## Crime Risk Rating System (1-100 Scale)

### Scale Interpretation
- **100**: Safer than 100% of U.S. neighborhoods (safest)
- **75-99**: Much safer than average
- **50-74**: Safer than average
- **25-49**: Less safe than average
- **1-24**: Much less safe than average
- **1**: Safer than just 1% of U.S. neighborhoods (least safe)

## Crime Categories

### Property Crimes
1. **Burglary**: Unlawful entry of a structure to commit a felony or theft
2. **Larceny-Theft**: Unlawful taking of property (excluding motor vehicles)
3. **Vehicle Theft**: Theft or attempted theft of a motor vehicle

### Violent Crimes
1. **Homicide**: Willful killing of one human being by another
2. **Rape**: All forms of non-consensual sexual penetration
3. **Armed Robbery**: Taking by force or threat of violence
4. **Aggravated Assault**: Attack for purpose of inflicting severe bodily injury

## Key Metrics to Report

### Primary Ratings
1. **Overall Crime Risk** (1-100 scale)
2. **Violent Crime Risk** (1-100 scale)
3. **Property Crime Risk** (1-100 scale)

### Additional Metrics
- Resident's chance of becoming a victim (1 in X)
- Crime rate per 1,000 residents
- Crime density per square mile
- Comparison to city average
- Comparison to national average

### Temporal Analysis
- Crime trends from 5 years ago to present
- Crime risk forecast 5 years into future
- Trend direction (improving, stable, declining)

## Analysis Guidelines

### Safety Categories
- **90-100**: Extremely safe, premium neighborhood
- **75-89**: Very safe, above-average safety
- **60-74**: Safe, typical suburban safety level
- **40-59**: Moderate risk, urban average
- **25-39**: Elevated risk, caution advised
- **1-24**: High risk, significant safety concerns

### Home Value Impact
- **High crime areas (1-30)**: Can reduce property values 10-30%
- **Average areas (40-60)**: Neutral impact
- **Low crime areas (75-100)**: Can increase property values 5-15%

### Key Questions to Answer
1. What is the overall crime risk rating?
2. How does violent crime compare to property crime?
3. What specific crime types are most prevalent?
4. Is crime improving or getting worse (trend analysis)?
5. How does it compare to city and national averages?
6. What are the chances of becoming a victim?

## Red Flags
- Crime risk below 30 (high risk)
- Violent crime significantly higher than property crime
- Declining crime trends (getting worse)
- Significantly higher than city/national averages
- Recent spikes in specific violent crimes

## Positive Indicators
- Crime risk above 75 (very safe)
- Improving crime trends over past 5 years
- Property crime higher than violent crime (less severe)
- Below city and national crime averages
- Strong community safety initiatives

## Context for Interpretation

### Property Crime vs. Violent Crime
- Property crime is more common but less severe
- Violent crime has greater impact on livability and value
- Areas with low violent crime but moderate property crime are generally acceptable

### Geographic Variation
- Crime can vary dramatically within same city
- Neighborhood-level data more important than city-level
- Adjacent neighborhoods can have very different profiles

### Buyer Considerations
**Families with children:**
- Prioritize low violent crime
- Acceptable property crime risk: 50+
- Acceptable violent crime risk: 70+

**Young professionals:**
- More tolerant of property crime
- Acceptable property crime risk: 40+
- Acceptable violent crime risk: 60+

**Investors:**
- Balance crime risk with rental demand
- Acceptable for rentals: Overall risk 40+
- Premium rentals: Overall risk 70+
